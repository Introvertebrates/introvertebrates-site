<h2>What was found</h2>

<p>Researchers described <em>Balticolasma wunderlichi</em> from two Eocene amber fossils: a male preserved in Baltic amber and a likely conspecific female from Rovno amber in northwestern Ukraine. The specimens are roughly 34–38 million years old.</p>

<p>The discovery represents the first known fossil member of Ortholasmatinae, a harvestman subfamily with no living representatives in Europe today.</p>

<h2>What makes that interesting</h2>

<p>Synchrotron-based imaging allowed the researchers to examine the fossils' three-dimensional morphology in unusually fine detail. The combination of characters may place the animal near the ancestral lineage of living ortholasmatines, although its precise relationship remains open to further study.</p>

<h2>Why it matters</h2>

<p>A first fossil record does more than add an extinct name. It changes the known age and former distribution of an entire lineage, and gives researchers a new point of comparison for understanding how modern harvestman diversity developed.</p>
