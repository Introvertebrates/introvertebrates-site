<h2>What was found</h2>

<p>Researchers described <em>Pikelinia floydmuraria</em>, a synanthropic crevice weaver from Tolima, Colombia. Its name combines a tribute to Pink Floyd with the spider's habit of living in wall crevices—a small arachnological riff on “Another Brick in the Wall.”</p>

<p>The paper describes males and females and includes dietary observations. The spiders captured a varied assortment of urban arthropods, including ants, flies, mosquitoes, beetles, and prey considerably larger than themselves.</p>

<h2>What makes that interesting</h2>

<p>The catchy name earned attention, but the ecology is the better story. Artificial lights and built structures may concentrate prey around the webs, allowing a tiny, easily overlooked predator to thrive alongside people.</p>

<h2>Why it matters</h2>

<p>This species broadens the idea of where discovery happens. Taxonomy is not confined to distant rainforest expeditions; meaningful new species and behavioural observations can be hiding in the cracks of familiar buildings.</p>
