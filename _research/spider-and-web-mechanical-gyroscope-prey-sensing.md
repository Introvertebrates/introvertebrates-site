<h2>A whole-body response to prey</h2>

<p>When prey strikes an orb web, the impact does more than vibrate individual threads. The study found characteristic pitching and rolling movements in the spider’s body. These movements varied with the location of the impact.</p>

<h2>The web helps organize the signal</h2>

<p>The researchers propose that the architecture and flexibility of the web transform an impact into useful whole-body motion. Instead of relying only on eight separate streams of vibration arriving through the legs, the spider may receive a simpler directional clue from the movement of its body within the web.</p>

<h2>Why this is interesting</h2>

<p>This makes the web look less like a passive trap and more like part of a distributed sensory system. Some information processing may occur through mechanics before the spider’s nervous system begins interpreting the signal.</p>

<h2>Important limitations</h2>

<p>The study demonstrates a plausible and robust mechanical information channel, but it does not directly show how the spider’s nervous system reads that channel. The live-animal observations involved adult female <i>Argiope trifasciata</i> under laboratory conditions, so the result should not yet be generalized to every orb-weaving spider.</p>
