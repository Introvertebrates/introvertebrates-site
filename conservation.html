Conservation

A life in care. A place in the wild.

The spiders we know by name belong to species with wild habitats, ecological roles and pressures of their own. Being familiar in collections does not tell us how a species is doing in nature.

Ruby

Chromatopelma cyaneopubescens

Endangered · Venezuela · 2015

Venezuela’s 2015 Red Book lists Ruby’s species as Endangered (EN). It identifies habitat loss and removal from the wild for trade as threats. This is a Venezuelan national assessment, not a global IUCN category.

Read the labels carefully

Risk, knowledge and protection.

Threatened has a specific meaning.

IUCN groups Vulnerable, Endangered and Critically Endangered as threatened. A national assessment describes risk within that country; a global assessment considers the species worldwide.

IUCN · Red List categories ↗

Trade listings answer another question.

A CITES Appendix II listing concerns regulation of international trade. It does not, on its own, mean a species is classified as Endangered. The extinction-risk category and the trade listing should be read separately.

CEC · official identification guide ↗

A missing label is not an all-clear.

This is a selection of documented assessments, not a complete status list for the collection. We leave uncertain categories unassigned. Informal hobby names also need a secure species identification before a species-level assessment can be attached.

From curiosity to care

Keep the wild part of the story.

Habitat protection and field research matter alongside good care in captivity. Our aim is to make the wild species visible behind the individual animal: where it lives, what threatens it, and what we still need to learn. Keeping a species is not, by itself, a conservation programme.
