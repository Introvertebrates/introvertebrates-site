# Spider anatomy photography wishlist

Updated 2026-09-19 after visually checking the dedicated anatomy folder, relevant collection/species images, and the wider Homepage photo archive. Image quality now takes priority over current-resident status; provenance and species identity still need to be recorded accurately.

This list is intentionally narrow. A subject belongs here only when the current repository cannot support the anatomy page clearly enough.

## Highest-value missing photographs

### 1. Clean ventral whole-spider view

**Why it matters:** The strongest genuine gap. One photograph could orient the sternum, labium, coxae, epigastric area, book-lung covers, and spinnerets in relation to each other.

**Shot brief:** Photograph a safely positioned animal through clean glass or a transparent catch cup. Keep the entire animal in frame, the body plane as parallel to the sensor as possible, and all underside landmarks unobstructed. Do not handle or invert an animal for the photograph.

### 2. Complete leg with all seven segments readable

**Why it matters:** Existing photographs contain excellent legs but not one unobstructed leg that can be followed confidently from coxa to tarsus.

**Shot brief:** A natural extended posture from above or slightly oblique. Preserve the body attachment and the foot in the same frame. A well-spread shed exoskeleton may be safer and more useful than a living subject.

### 3. Mature male palpal organs

**Why it matters:** The sexual-anatomy section currently has no diagnostic repository photograph.

**Shot brief:** Frontal or ventrolateral macro of both pedipalp tips on a confirmed mature male. Record the individual and species. Avoid labeling fine structures unless identification is checked against a reliable species-level source.

### 4. Female exuvium showing spermathecae

**Why it matters:** This would explain why a shed exoskeleton can be more informative for sexing than a general body photograph.

**Shot brief:** Safely soften and open an intact abdominal portion of a molt, then photograph the internal surface square-on with even, diffuse light and a scale. Record the individual, species, and molt date separately from the public image.

### 5. Araneomorph comparison set

**Why it matters:** The collection is photographically mygalomorph-heavy. The current `Araniella` image is a good real-world representative, but not a controlled anatomy plate.

**Shot brief:** Dorsal, frontal, and—only if available without harm—ventral views of the same ordinary araneomorph. A larger house, orb-weaver, wolf, or fishing spider will make structures easier to read. Release the animal after photographing it.

## Valuable supporting photographs

### 6. Tarsus, claws, and adhesive hairs

An extreme macro of the last leg segment, terminal claws, and any claw tufts/scopulae that are genuinely visible. A molt under magnification may give a cleaner, safer result than a living spider.

### 7. Unobstructed spinnerets and spigots

The checked archive has excellent webbing photographs but no clean macro that resolves the spinnerets, much less individual spigots. A clean posterior macro with the spinnerets separated from the background would materially improve the section.

### 8. Tibial mating structures where actually present

Photograph only a confirmed mature male of a taxon known to have the structures. Include the relevant first-leg tibia and body context; do not treat absence in a photograph as proof that a species lacks them.

### 9. More formal dorsal whole-body plate

The current full-body images are visually strong, but none combines a directly overhead sensor plane, separated legs, clean background, and sufficient depth of field. Useful, but lower priority than the ventral view and leg series.

## Already covered well enough

Do not schedule new photographs specifically for these topics unless an unusually good opportunity appears:

- eye clusters;
- exposed chelicerae and fangs;
- ordinary pedipalps;
- general setae and surface texture;
- layered webbing and silk in use;
- ventral book-lung regions on a mygalomorph;
- a molt in progress;
- whole-body tarantula portraits.
