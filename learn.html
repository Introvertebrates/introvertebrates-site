Two major lineages

Tarantulas are not less “true” than other spiders.

“True spider” is sometimes used informally for Araneomorphae, but it can give the wrong impression. Tarantulas, many trapdoor spiders, and their mygalomorph relatives are every bit as much members of the spider order Araneae.

Mygalomorphae

Tarantulas, many trapdoor spiders, funnel-web spiders, and relatives

Chelicerae
Arranged roughly parallel to one another rather than opposing like pincers.
Respiration
Usually two pairs of book lungs.
Silk and lifestyle
Many are sedentary burrow or retreat dwellers. Their silk may look simple, but it can extend the animal’s sensory world and play a direct role in prey capture.

Araneomorphae

Jumping spiders, wolf spiders, orb weavers, house spiders, and most described spiders

Chelicerae
Oppose one another, producing the familiar inward, pincer-like action.
Respiration
Most retain one anterior pair of book lungs, with the posterior pair replaced by tracheae.
Silk and lifestyle
An exceptionally diverse lineage, ranging from elaborate web builders to active hunters that use little more than safety lines and retreats.
Better than “primitive”: mygalomorphs are an early-branching living lineage that retains several ancestral features. They are modern animals with their own long evolutionary history, not unfinished versions of araneomorphs. The illustrations above are explanatory comparisons, not to scale, and cannot show the variation within either lineage.

Read the primary literature: Opatova et al. - Mygalomorphae phylogeny and evolution; Garrison et al. - the spider tree of life; Sharma - book-lung evolution; Hedin et al. - a tarantula genome compared across spider evolution.
