Natural history.

Habitat & lifestyle

A terrestrial Mexican tarantula associated with seasonal dry forest. Shelter can be excavated or adapted beneath rocks, roots, and other cover.

What to notice

Red-orange flames rise from the patellae against a dark body. The pattern is more sharply localised than the broader orange bands of some relatives.

Archive status

The photograph is mine and the animal was once in my care. It is not a current resident and no current Codex statistics are attached to this profile.

Taxonomy and range

The World Spider Catalog recognises Brachypelma auratum Schmidt, 1992 as an accepted species from Mexico. Conservation guidance places it in the Balsas River Basin, a biologically distinctive region of western and central Mexico.

Burrows, shelter, and activity

Field and conservation accounts describe a ground-dwelling, burrow-associated spider in Balsas dry forest. It can modify natural cavities beneath rocks and tree roots or excavate a retreat, emerging primarily when conditions and prey activity are favourable.

Feeding ecology

Like other large terrestrial theraphosids, it is an opportunistic ambush predator. Vibration and close-range sensory information help it respond to small animals moving near the retreat rather than relying on long visual pursuit.

Conservation and trade

The species is covered by CITES Appendix II controls on international trade. An official identification guide also reports a 2018 Near Threatened assessment, reflecting its restricted range and pressure from habitat change and collection.
