Natural history.

Habitat & lifestyle

A northern South American tarantula in the arboreal genus Psalmopoeus. Clara’s photographs document her use of vertical bark and foliage in this collection. [1] [2]

What to notice

Orange markings contrast with the dark body. Clara’s photographs show the leg and abdominal pattern among bark and foliage.

In this collection

Clara has vertical structure, a secure retreat, and foliage for cover. Her feeding and molt observations are presented as an individual record below.

Taxonomy and range

Psalmopoeus irminia is an accepted theraphosid recorded by the World Spider Catalog from Venezuela, Guyana and Brazil. Research on the genus describes Psalmopoeus as an arboreal lineage, providing the broader context for Clara’s use of vertical cover. [1] [2]

Burrows, shelter, and activity

Clara’s photographs and collection record show vertical structure, foliage and a concealed retreat. Research on Psalmopoeus provides an arboreal comparison at genus level. Her use of bark, cover and silk is followed as an individual observation. [2]

Feeding ecology

General spider biology explains how movement on a surface or silk can supply prey cues. Applying that to Clara’s concealed vertical routes is a keeper interpretation, not a species-specific field diet study. Her feeding responses and outcomes remain part of her individual record. [3]

Defence, senses, and movement

Psalmopoeus does not have urticating hairs. Speed, concealment, and a defensive posture when cornered make opening the enclosure the highest-risk moment, not the animal sitting quietly inside its retreat. [4]

Keeper interpretation

Vertical structure should connect all the way to the substrate, with a narrow retreat, foliage, water, and good airflow. That gives Clara several concealed routes and reduces the need to cross exposed glass during routine activity.

Conservation context

Status, trade & uncertainty.

IUCN evaluates extinction risk; CITES regulates international trade. They answer different questions, and missing data should remain visible. [5] [6]
