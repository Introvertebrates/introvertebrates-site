Idiothele mira

Blue-foot baboon tarantula

Meet Helle, the Idiothele mira in the Introvertebrates collection. This South African tarantula is known for silk-lined retreats that can close with a delicate trapdoor.

Natural history.

Habitat & lifestyle

The original description reports densely silk-lined tunnels or cells beneath rocks and logs in lightly wooded habitats. A thin silk door can conceal the entrance.

What to notice

The blue distal leg segments help distinguish the species. The silk door can be inconspicuous in the field; not seeing one does not establish that a retreat lacks a closure.

In this collection

This profile separates the current resident’s documented history from species-level research. The portrait shows Helle.

Taxonomy and range

Idiothele mira Gallon, 2010 is an accepted South African theraphosid. The original description records material from Ndumo and Tembe in northeastern South Africa and distinguishes it from Idiothele nigrofulva.

Burrows, shelter, and activity

Gallon’s field account places retreats under rocks and logs. The entrance can be sealed with a thin, wafer-like silk trapdoor, a useful reminder that elaborate shelter is not restricted to spiders commonly called trapdoor spiders.

Feeding ecology

A concealed retreat is compatible with a predatory lifestyle. Helle’s prey choices and feeding responses belong in the individual collection record; they cannot be inferred from the taxonomic description.

Defence, senses, and movement

The description documents specialised sensory hairs and structures on the limbs and mouthparts. Retreat architecture offers another way to understand the animal without repeatedly opening or disturbing its shelter.

From natural history to husbandry

A secure retreat and material suitable for silk attachment follow from the published habitat observations. This is a natural-history inference, not evidence that Helle has already built a particular structure.
